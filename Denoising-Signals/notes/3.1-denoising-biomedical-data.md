## 🧹 **5. Median Filter for Subthreshold Values**

### Code

```python
threshold = 321

subthreshold_indices = np.where(df['filtered'] < threshold)[0]

k = 7
for i in subthreshold_indices:
    df['filtered'][i] = np.median(df['filtered'][np.max((0,i-k)):np.min((len(df),i+k))])
```

### What this does

#### **Step A — Identify “small” values**

Define a threshold:

- Any sample **below 321** is considered suspicious (likely noise or dropouts).

#### **Step B — Apply a median filter only to those points**

For each index where the signal is below the threshold:

- You take a **window of 7 samples on each side**  
- Compute the **median** of that window  
- Replace the noisy value with the median

### Why median filtering?

Median filters are excellent for removing:

- impulse noise  
- spikes  
- sudden dropouts  

They preserve edges better than mean filters.

### Result

The filtered signal now has fewer sudden dips or spikes.

## 🧹 **7. Mean Filter (Moving Average)**

### Code

```python
k = 7
for i in range(len(df)):
    df['filtered'][i] = np.mean(df['filtered'][np.max((0,i-k)):np.min((len(df),i+k))])
```

### What this does

This applies a **moving average filter** across the entire signal:

- For each sample `i`, you take a window of ±7 samples  
- Compute the **mean**  
- Replace the value with that mean  

### Why mean filtering?

A moving average:

- smooths the waveform  
- reduces high‑frequency noise  
- makes the ECG curve more stable  

It’s a gentle low‑pass filter.

### Result

Your ECG becomes smoother and more readable, especially around the P‑Q‑R‑S‑T waves.
