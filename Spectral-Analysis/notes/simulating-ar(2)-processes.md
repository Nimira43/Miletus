# 📘 Simulating AR(2) Processes — Behaviour of Different Coefficients

This document summarises the behaviour of five autoregressive simulations of order 2:

\[
x_t = \alpha_1 x_{t-1} + \alpha_2 x_{t-2} + \epsilon_t
\]

where:

- \(\alpha = [\alpha_1, \alpha_2]\)
- \(k = 2\)
- \(\epsilon_t\) is Gaussian noise (either full noise or scaled noise)

***Please refer to the notebook titled "Simultating_AR2_Processes.ipynb" for code and plots.***

---

## 🧪 Example 1 — **α = [-0.6, 0.9]**  

Noise: **full** (`np.random.randn()`)

### **Behaviour**

- The combination of a **negative first coefficient** and a **large positive second coefficient** creates **oscillatory behaviour**.
- Because the noise is full‑strength, the oscillations are **rough**, with visible randomness.
- The system is close to the stability boundary, so the oscillations may grow or shrink irregularly.

### **Interpretation**

This AR(2) setup behaves like a **damped but noisy oscillator** — flipping sign due to the negative α₁, while α₂ keeps the oscillation alive.

---

## 🧪 Example 2 — **α = [0.6, 0.9]**  

Noise: **full**

### **Behaviour**

- Both coefficients are positive, so the process tends to **reinforce its previous direction**.
- The large α₂ = 0.9 creates **strong persistence**.
- The result is a **smooth, trending signal** with long swings upward or downward.
- Still noisy due to full Gaussian noise.

### **Interpretation**

This is a **persistent, trending AR(2)** process — less oscillatory, more “momentum‑driven”.

---

## 🧪 Example 3 — **α = [-0.8, 0.9]**  

Noise: **very small** (`np.random.randn() / 50`)

### **Behaviour**

- Strong negative α₁ combined with strong positive α₂ creates **clean oscillations**.
- Because noise is tiny, the oscillations are **smooth and regular**.
- This is close to a **deterministic AR(2) oscillator**.

### **Interpretation**

This example shows the **pure oscillatory nature** of AR(2) when noise is minimal.  
It resembles a **sinusoid generated by recursion**.

---

## 🧪 Example 4 — **α = [-0.08, 0.9]**  

Noise: **very small**

### **Behaviour**

- α₁ is small and negative → only a slight sign‑flipping influence.
- α₂ = 0.9 dominates → strong persistence.
- With tiny noise, the signal becomes a **slow, smooth drift** with mild oscillatory tendencies.

### **Interpretation**

This is a **weak oscillator** — mostly persistent, slightly wobbly, very smooth.

---

## 🧪 Example 5 — **α = [0, 1.9]**  

Noise: **very small**

### **Behaviour**

- α₂ = 1.9 is **far above the stability limit** (for AR(2), stability requires roots outside the unit circle).
- This produces a **divergent process** — values grow rapidly.
- Even tiny noise gets amplified.
- The signal will **explode**, often exponentially.

### **Interpretation**

This is an **unstable AR(2)** system.  
It demonstrates how autoregressive models can blow up when coefficients exceed stability bounds.

---

## 📈 Summary of Outcomes

| Example | α-values | Noise | Behaviour | Interpretation |
| -------- | ---------- | -------- | ----------- | ---------------- |
| **1** | [-0.6, 0.9] | full | noisy oscillations | damped but chaotic oscillator |
| **2** | [0.6, 0.9] | full | smooth trends, persistent | momentum-driven AR(2) |
| **3** | [-0.8, 0.9] | tiny | clean oscillations | near-deterministic oscillator |
| **4** | [-0.08, 0.9] | tiny | slow drift, mild wobble | weak oscillator, high persistence |
| **5** | [0, 1.9] | tiny | explosive growth | unstable AR(2) |

---

## 🎯 Key Takeaways

- **Negative α₁** → oscillations  
- **Large α₂** → persistence or instability  
- **Small noise** → reveals the underlying deterministic structure  
- **Large noise** → masks the structure with randomness  
- **α₂ > 1** → instability and divergence  

The experiments illustrate the full spectrum of AR(2) behaviour:  
from stable oscillations → smooth trends → explosive instability.
